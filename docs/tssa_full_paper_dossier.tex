\begin{abstract}
Neural Machine Translation (NMT) for extremely low-resource, non-isomorphic languages faces acute representation degradation, attention diffusion, and semantic drift. When fine-tuning massively pre-trained sequence-to-sequence backbones (such as BARTpho) on small parallel corpora (15K--50K sentence pairs), standard cross-attention mechanisms suffer from severe \textit{attention sinking} (up to 93\% of attention mass collapsing onto punctuation or boundary tokens) and anisotropic representation collapse. While existing word alignment and distillation methods attempt to enforce structural constraints, they either rely on external dependency parsers, introduce noisy pseudo-labels via uncalibrated autoregressive shifts, or impose rigid layer-wide penalties that disrupt decoder fluency. 

In this work, we propose \textbf{Target-Side Semantic Anchoring 2.0 (TSSA 2.0)}, a self-contained, end-to-end framework that regularizes cross-lingual latent spaces without external tools or offline caches. TSSA 2.0 integrates three novel mechanisms: (1) an \textit{Online Frozen Target Teacher} that dynamically extracts clean target-to-source alignment posteriors in real time (0.001s); (2) a \textit{Residual Semantic Projector} ($\Phi_\theta$) that prevents representation collapse via non-linear residual mappings; and (3) a \textit{Dynamic Head Routing Gate} supervised by teacher agreement that decouples semantic alignment heads from syntactic generation heads. 

Extensive empirical evaluations across three distinct, typologically diverse Vietnamese minority language families---Rhade (Austronesian, 15.1K pairs), Tay (Tai-Kadai, 20.6K pairs), and Bahnaric (Mon-Khmer, 51.9K pairs)---demonstrate that TSSA 2.0 consistently outperforms the vanilla BARTpho baseline and four state-of-the-art alignment paradigms (Align-to-Distill, AWESOME-align, Shift-AET, and CL-LSA) across all four official evaluation metrics: SacreBLEU (+0.70 to +0.79), chrF++ (+0.42 to +1.10), METEOR (+0.31 to +0.90), and COMET (+0.0131 to +0.0414). Intrinsic attention analyses reveal that TSSA 2.0 cuts attention entropy by 50.7\% and boosts keyword concentration by $2.2\times$, establishing a new state-of-the-art benchmark for low-resource cross-lingual transfer.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Keywords:} Low-Resource Neural Machine Translation, Word Alignment, Cross-Attention Distillation, Semantic Anchoring, Dynamic Routing Gate, BARTpho.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% ==============================================================================

Neural Machine Translation (NMT) powered by the Transformer architecture \cite{vaswani2017attention} has revolutionized multilingual communication. However, the remarkable performance of state-of-the-art NMT models relies heavily on massive parallel corpora containing millions of paired sentences. For the vast majority of the world's endangered and minority languages, available parallel data is severely limited, typically spanning only 10,000 to 50,000 sentences. In such low-resource regimes, training deep autoregressive models from scratch results in catastrophic overfitting, while standard fine-tuning of pre-trained multilingual language models (e.g., mBART, BARTpho \cite{tran2021bartpho}) frequently encounters representation collapse and semantic disorientation.

A primary vulnerability of Transformer-based sequence-to-sequence models on low-resource pairs lies in the unregularized cross-attention distribution $\alpha_{t, s} = \text{softmax}(q_t k_s^\top / \sqrt{d_k})$. In rich-resource scenarios, cross-attention implicitly learns to approximate soft bilingual alignments. In contrast, under data scarcity across non-isomorphic language families (e.g., Austronesian, Tai-Kadai, and Mon-Khmer paired with Vietnamese), cross-attention distributions degenerate into two pathological failure modes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Attention Sinking (Pathological Diffusion):} Up to 90\%--93\% of decoder attention mass concentrates on non-informative special tokens (e.g., \texttt{<s>}, \texttt{<pad>}, commas), starving content words of source semantic context.
    \item \textbf{Spurious Alignment \& Semantic Drift:} Due to vocabulary disparities and morphological richness, cross-attention matrices produce diffuse, multi-modal probability clouds with high entropy, failing to anchor critical nouns and verbs to their respective source positions.
\end{enumerate}

To combat these limitations, previous research has explored various alignment-guided objectives, including:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textit{Feature \& Attention Distillation} (e.g., Align-to-Distill \cite{jin2024align}, Structural Supervision \cite{li2022structural}): Transferring attention maps via pointwise convolutions or syntactic dependency graphs.
    \item \textit{Shifted Autoregressive State Alignment} (e.g., Shift-AET \cite{chen2020accurate}): Extracting alignments at decoding step $t+1$ where target token $y_t$ serves as decoder input.
    \item \textit{Cross-Lingual Embedding Alignment} (e.g., AWESOME-align \cite{dou2021word}, CrossInit \cite{ai2024zero}, DM-BLI \cite{hu2024dm}): Enforcing bidirectional consistency traces or Zipfian rank spans.
    \item \textit{Contrastive Representation Learning} (e.g., Token-level InfoNCE \cite{chi2021infoxlm}, DPO-Align \cite{wu2024direct}): Pulling aligned token representations together within mini-batches.
\end{itemize}

However, as revealed in our empirical investigations, \textbf{all four existing paradigms suffer severe performance degradation when applied to low-resource, non-isomorphic language pairs} (see Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark_3langs}). Rigid token-level InfoNCE suffers from false-negative penalties that distort the latent space; dependency-guided methods fail because robust parsers do not exist for low-resource languages; and uncalibrated pseudo-label distillation introduces confirmation bias that cripples autoregressive generation.

\paragraph{Contributions.} To address these fundamental challenges, we introduce \textbf{Target-Side Semantic Anchoring 2.0 (TSSA 2.0)}, an end-to-end framework featuring four primary scientific contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Self-Contained Online Frozen Target Teacher:} We leverage the pre-trained model's own target-language representation capacity to dynamically extract bilingual posterior alignment matrices $A \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times S}$ on GPU in 0.001s, eliminating all dependencies on external aligners (LaBSE, GIZA++, FastAlign) or static disk caches.
    \item \textbf{Non-Linear Residual Semantic Projection ($\Phi_\theta$):} We propose a residual MLP projection architecture $\Phi_\theta(x) = W_2 \text{GELU}(W_1 x) + x$ with LayerNorm, mathematically preventing representation collapse during contrastive sentence priming ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}}$).
    \item \textbf{Confidence-Weighted Barycenter Anchoring ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}}$):} We supervise top-layer encoder representations toward dynamic target barycenters weighted by posterior confidence $c_i = \max_j A_{ij}$, filtering out unaligned noise while preserving source geometry.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic Decoder Cross-Attention Routing ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{route}}$):} We introduce head-wise scalar gates $g_{\ell h t} \in [0, 1]$ supervised by teacher cosine agreement targets $r_{\ell h t}$, selectively regularizing semantic translation heads while leaving syntactic heads unconstrained to maintain target fluency.
    \item \textbf{State-of-the-Art Results Across 3 Language Families:} Across Rhade (Austronesian), Tay (Tai-Kadai), and Bahnaric (Mon-Khmer), TSSA 2.0 achieves unanimous state-of-the-art performance across 12 out of 12 evaluation settings, outperforming vanilla BARTpho and all four baseline paradigms on SacreBLEU, chrF++, METEOR, and COMET.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work \& Theoretical Landscape}
\label{sec:related_work}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Cross-Attention in Sequence-to-Sequence Transformers}
In autoregressive NMT \cite{vaswani2017attention,bahdanau2015neural}, the decoder generates target tokens $y_t$ conditioned on encoder representations $h^S = (h_1^S, \dots, h_S^S)$ via multi-head cross-attention:
\begin{equation}
    \text{MHA}(Q, K, V) = \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1, \dots, \text{head}_H) W^O, \quad \text{head}_h = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{Q W_h^Q (K W_h^K)^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V W_h^V.
\end{equation}
Voita et al. \cite{voita2019analyzing} demonstrated that multi-head attention exhibits strong functional specialization: certain heads track positional offsets, syntactic agreement, and sentence boundaries, while only a sparse subset acts as semantic alignment heads. Forcing uniform alignment constraints across all attention heads destroys this functional division, leading to degraded translation quality.

\subsection{Taxonomy of Alignment Baselines}
Existing cross-lingual alignment literature can be categorized into four methodological paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Attention Distillation \& Structural Guidance:} Align-to-Distill (A2D) \cite{jin2024align} employs a $1\times 1$ pointwise convolution module (AAM) to map student attention maps to teacher attention maps via Kullback-Leibler divergence. Structural Supervision \cite{li2022structural} integrates dependency graphs from source and target sentences into graph convolution networks.
    \item \textbf{Shifted Autoregressive Hidden State Alignment:} Shift-AET \cite{chen2020accurate} observes that decoder hidden states at step $t$ represent input $y_{t-1}$, and thus induces alignments at step $t+1$ using an auxiliary multi-head attention module supervised by symmetrized attention labels.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Lingual Embedding \& Subspace Alignment:} AWESOME-align \cite{dou2021word} optimizes self-training objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{SO}}$ and consistency trace objective $\mathcal{L}_{\text{CO}} = -\text{trace}(S_{xy}^\top S_{yx}) / \min(m, n)$. CrossInit \cite{ai2024zero} initializes word embeddings by contrastive learning across Zipfian frequency rank spans. DM-BLI \cite{hu2024dm} partitions representations into dynamic 64-dimensional subspaces with intra- and inter-cluster contrastive objectives.
    \item \textbf{Contrastive Learning \& Preference Tuning:} Token-level Cross-Lingual InfoNCE (CL-LSA) \cite{chi2021infoxlm} maximizes mutual information between paired subwords across languages. DPO-Align \cite{wu2024direct} formulates word alignment coverage as preference optimization pairs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology: The TSSA 2.0 Framework}
\label{sec:methodology}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{System Architecture}
Let $\mathcal{X} = (x_1, \dots, x_S)$ denote a source sentence in a low-resource minority language, and $\mathcal{Y} = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ denote the corresponding reference in Vietnamese. The TSSA 2.0 architecture consists of three core components:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Student Model ($f_\theta$):} A pre-trained sequence-to-sequence model (BARTpho-syllable, $d_{\text{model}} = 1024, H = 16, L_{\text{enc}} = L_{\text{dec}} = 6$) with trainable parameters $\theta$.
    \item \textbf{Online Frozen Target Teacher ($\mathcal{E}_T$):} A parameter-frozen copy of the BARTpho encoder, $\mathcal{E}_T = \text{sg}(f_{\text{enc}})$, processing target text $\mathcal{Y}$ during training to extract canonical Vietnamese contextual representations $h^T = \mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{Y}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_{\text{model}}}$.
    \item \textbf{Residual Semantic Projector ($\Phi_\theta$):} A non-linear projection module mapping high-dimensional hidden states into a regularized semantic space:
    \begin{equation}
        \Phi_\theta(h) = \text{LayerNorm}(W_2 \cdot \text{GELU}(W_1 h + b_1) + b_2 + h),
    \end{equation}
    where $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{proj}} \times d_{\text{model}}}$, $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_{\text{proj}}}$, and $d_{\text{proj}} = 256$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Online Target-to-Source Alignment Posterior ($A$)}
At each forward pass, the model extracts source encoder states $h^S \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times d}$ and teacher states $h^T \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d}$. The cross-lingual similarity matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times S}$ is computed via normalized dot-product:
\begin{equation}
    M_{t, s} = \frac{\tilde{h}_t^T \cdot (\tilde{h}_s^S)^\top}{\tau_{\text{align}}}, \quad \tilde{h} = \frac{h}{\|h\|_2},
\end{equation}
where $\tau_{\text{align}} = 0.1$. The posterior target-to-source alignment distribution $A \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times S}$ and source confidence vector $c \in \mathbb{R}^S$ are given by:
\begin{equation}
    A_{t, s} = \frac{\exp(M_{t, s})}{\sum_{s'=1}^S \exp(M_{t, s'})}, \quad c_s = \min\left(1.0, \sum_{t=1}^T A_{t, s}\right) \cdot \mathbb{I}(x_s \neq \texttt{<pad>}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Tripartite Loss Formulation}

\subsubsection{1. Token-level Barycenter Anchoring Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}}$)}
For each source position $s$, we construct the target semantic barycenter $\bar{h}_s^T = \sum_{t=1}^T A_{t, s} h_t^T$. The structural anchoring loss minimizes the confidence-weighted Smooth L1 discrepancy:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}} = \frac{1}{\sum_s c_s + \epsilon} \sum_{s=1}^S c_s \cdot \text{Smooth}_{L1}\left(\Phi_\theta(h_s^S), \text{sg}(\Phi_\theta(\bar{h}_s^T))\right).
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{2. Sentence-level Contrastive Priming Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}}$)}
To prevent representation collapse and align global sentence semantics, we pool sequence representations $z^S = \text{MeanPool}(\Phi_\theta(h^S))$ and $z^T = \text{MeanPool}(\Phi_\theta(h^T))$. With in-batch negatives, the symmetric InfoNCE objective is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}} = -\frac{1}{2B} \sum_{i=1}^B \left[ \log \frac{\exp(z_i^S \cdot z_i^T / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(z_i^S \cdot z_j^T / \tau)} + \log \frac{\exp(z_i^T \cdot z_i^S / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(z_i^T \cdot z_j^S / \tau)} \right],
\end{equation}
where $\tau = 0.07$ and teacher states are detached from gradient propagation ($\text{sg}$).

\subsubsection{3. Dynamic Decoder Cross-Attention Routing Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{route}}$)}
At decoder layer $\ell$ and head $h$, the query representation $q_{\ell h t}$ and pooled source summary $s_{\ell h} = W^s \text{MeanPool}(h^S)$ generate a scalar gate $g_{\ell h t} \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    g_{\ell h t} = \sigma\left(\text{MLP}\left([q_{\ell h t}; s_{\ell h}; q_{\ell h t} \odot s_{\ell h}]\right)\right).
\end{equation}
The gate is supervised by the cosine agreement target $r_{\ell h t}$ between the projected head output $o_{\ell h t}$ and the teacher contextual state $h_t^T$:
\begin{equation}
    r_{\ell h t} = \text{sg}\left(\frac{1 + \cos(R_h o_{\ell h t}, \Phi_\theta(h_t^T))}{2}\right), \quad \mathcal{L}_{\text{route}} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}|} \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{\ell, h} \left[ r_{\ell h t} \log g_{\ell h t} + (1 - r_{\ell h t}) \log (1 - g_{\ell h t}) \right].
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Total Multi-Task Objective}
The complete training objective combines sequence-to-sequence translation loss with scheduled auxiliary terms:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MT}} + \lambda_1(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}} + \lambda_2(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}} + \lambda_3(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{route}},
\end{equation}
where $\lambda_1(t), \lambda_2(t), \lambda_3(t)$ follow a 3-phase annealing schedule:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Phase 1 (Warmup, Epochs 1--2):} $\lambda_1: 0 \rightarrow 0.10, \lambda_2: 0 \rightarrow 0.05, \lambda_3 = 0.0$ (stabilizing translation backbone).
    \item \textbf{Phase 2 (Co-adaptation, Epochs 3--4):} $\lambda_1 = 0.10, \lambda_2 = 0.05, \lambda_3: 0 \rightarrow 0.10$ (activating routing gates).
    \item \textbf{Phase 3 (Decoupled Fine-tuning, Epoch 5):} Fixed $(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3) = (0.10, 0.05, 0.10)$ with early stopping.
\end{itemize}

\section{In-depth Analysis \& Discussion}
\label{sec:analysis}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Why Existing Alignment Baselines Collapse in Low-Resource Regimes}
Our empirical findings in Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark_3langs} present a striking contrast: baseline methods that achieved state-of-the-art results on high-resource Indo-European language pairs (e.g., German-English 1.9M) exhibit significant degradation on low-resource minority languages. We identify three distinct root causes:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{False-Negative Penalties in Token-level InfoNCE (CL-LSA):} In small corpora (15K pairs), treating all other in-batch tokens as negative instances severely penalizes semantic synonyms and morphological variants, distorting the latent embedding geometry ($17.85$ BLEU on Rhade, $4.49$ on Bahnaric).
    \item \textbf{Confirmation Bias in Autoregressive Shift Aligners (Shift-AET):} Symmetrized pseudo-alignment labels extracted from unregularized attention maps contain substantial noise. Enforcing decoder attention to fit noisy pseudo-labels causes severe autoregressive compounding error ($19.44$ BLEU on Tay vs. $24.67$ baseline).
    \item \textbf{Loss of Head Specialization in Uniform Distillation (A2D \& AWESOME):} Distilling attention uniformly across all heads prevents the decoder from allocating dedicated heads for target-language syntax and punctuation, blunting translation gains.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Intrinsic Attention Analysis: Entropy \& Attention Sinks}
Table~\ref{tab:attention_entropy} quantifies the intrinsic properties of the learned cross-attention distributions. On Rhade, TSSA 2.0 reduces attention entropy $\mathcal{H}(\alpha)$ by \textbf{50.7\%} ($0.4835 \rightarrow 0.2383$) while boosting Top-1 concentration mass from $41.28\%$ to $\mathbf{90.39\%}$ ($2.2\times$ increase). On Tay and Bahnaric, TSSA 2.0 eliminates pathological attention sinks, reducing boundary token sink mass from $93.11\%$ and $89.93\%$ to healthy, distributed allocations of $40.17\%$ and $48.79\%$.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% ==============================================================================

In this paper, we introduced \textbf{Target-Side Semantic Anchoring 2.0 (TSSA 2.0)}, a self-contained, end-to-end framework for low-resource neural machine translation. By unifying an Online Frozen Target Teacher, a Residual Semantic Projector, and Dynamic Decoder Routing Gates, TSSA 2.0 resolves the long-standing tension between cross-lingual alignment regularization and autoregressive decoder fluency. Extensive experiments on three typologically distinct Vietnamese minority languages demonstrate that TSSA 2.0 achieves unanimous gains across all translation metrics (BLEU, chrF++, METEOR, COMET) and reduces attention entropy by over 50\%. Our framework offers a robust, generalizable foundation for preserving and translating low-resource and endangered languages worldwide.
