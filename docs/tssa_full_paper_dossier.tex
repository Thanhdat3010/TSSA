\begin{abstract}
This technical report documents the theoretical formulation, architectural design, and empirical validation of \textbf{Target-Side Semantic Anchoring 2.0 (TSSA 2.0)} for low-resource Neural Machine Translation (NMT). When fine-tuning massively pre-trained sequence-to-sequence backbones (BARTpho) on low-resource, typologically distant minority language pairs (15K--50K sentence pairs), standard cross-attention mechanisms suffer from severe attention sinking (up to 93\% of attention mass collapsing onto boundary tokens) and anisotropic representation distortion. Existing alignment techniques (e.g., token-level InfoNCE, autoregressive shift alignment, dependency graph supervision) degrade significantly in low-resource settings due to false-negative penalties, noisy pseudo-label confirmation bias, and lack of parser support.

TSSA 2.0 provides an end-to-end, self-contained solution without requiring external parsers, statistical alignment tools, or offline disk caches. It unifies three core mechanisms: (1) an \textit{Online Frozen Target Teacher} extracting clean target-to-source alignment posteriors in real time (0.001s); (2) a \textit{Residual Semantic Projector} ($\Phi_\theta$) preserving latent geometry via non-linear residual mappings; and (3) a \textit{Dynamic Head Routing Gate} supervised by teacher agreement that decouples semantic translation heads from syntactic generation heads. 

Empirical evaluations across three distinct Vietnamese minority language families---Rhade (Austronesian, 15.1K pairs), Tay (Tai-Kadai, 20.6K pairs), and Bahnaric (Mon-Khmer, 51.9K pairs)---demonstrate that TSSA 2.0 consistently outperforms the vanilla BARTpho baseline and four representative alignment paradigms (Align-to-Distill, AWESOME-align, Shift-AET, and CL-LSA) across all four standard metrics: SacreBLEU (+0.70 to +0.79), chrF++ (+0.42 to +1.10), METEOR (+0.31 to +0.90), and COMET (+0.0131 to +0.0414). Intrinsic attention analysis confirms a 50.7\% reduction in cross-attention entropy and a $2.2\times$ increase in semantic keyword concentration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Formulation \& Core Challenges}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}
% ==============================================================================

In low-resource neural machine translation, we are given a parallel dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathcal{X}^{(k)}, \mathcal{Y}^{(k)})\}_{k=1}^{|\mathcal{D}|}$, where $\mathcal{X} = (x_1, \dots, x_S)$ is a source sentence in an under-resourced minority language and $\mathcal{Y} = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ is the target sentence in Vietnamese. The parallel corpus size $|\mathcal{D}|$ is extremely constrained (ranging from 15,000 to 52,000 sentence pairs).

Under these data-scarce conditions and across non-isomorphic language families (Austronesian, Tai-Kadai, and Mon-Khmer paired with Vietnamese), standard sequence-to-sequence fine-tuning faces three fundamental challenges:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Attention Sinking \& Contextual Starvation:} Standard cross-attention $\alpha_{t, s} = \text{softmax}(q_t k_s^\top / \sqrt{d_k})$ degenerates such that up to 90\%--93\% of attention mass is absorbed by special delimiter tokens (\texttt{<s>}, \texttt{<pad>}), leaving content words ungrounded.
    \item \textbf{Representation Collapse in High-Dimensional Spaces:} High-dimensional embeddings ($d_{\text{model}} = 1024$) fine-tuned on limited samples undergo anisotropic collapse, where sentence representations occupy a narrow cone in representation space.
    \item \textbf{Failure of Existing Alignment Paradigms on Low-Resource Data:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Token-level InfoNCE (CL-LSA):} Treating all other in-batch tokens as negative instances severely penalizes morphological variants and synonyms (false negative penalty), degrading performance ($17.85$ BLEU on Rhade, $4.49$ on Bahnaric).
        \item \textit{Shifted Autoregressive States (Shift-AET):} Symmetrized pseudo-alignment labels extracted from unregularized models contain substantial noise, causing error propagation during autoregressive decoding ($19.44$ BLEU on Tay vs. $24.67$ baseline).
        \item \textit{Syntactic Supervision:} Low-resource minority languages lack robust dependency parsers, introducing corrupted graph structures.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Architecture: TSSA 2.0}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
% ==============================================================================

TSSA 2.0 addresses these challenges through a fully integrated, multi-task sequence-to-sequence framework built on top of the pre-trained BARTpho-syllable backbone ($d_{\text{model}} = 1024, H = 16, L_{\text{enc}} = L_{\text{dec}} = 6$).

\subsection{1. Online Frozen Target Teacher ($\mathcal{E}_T$)}
Instead of relying on external aligners (LaBSE, GIZA++, FastAlign) or static disk caches, TSSA 2.0 embeds a parameter-frozen copy of the target language encoder:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_T = \text{stop\_gradient}(f_{\text{enc}}), \quad h^T = \mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{Y}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_{\text{model}}}.
\end{equation}
Because the student decoder and teacher encoder share the identical Vietnamese tokenizer and vocabulary, target position $t$ in the teacher maps directly to position $t$ in the decoder without tokenization mismatches.

\subsection{2. Dynamic Posterior Alignment Matrix ($A$)}
During training, the student encoder outputs source states $h^S = f_{\text{enc}}(\mathcal{X}) \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times d_{\text{model}}}$. The cross-lingual token similarity matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times S}$ is computed on-the-fly:
\begin{equation}
    M_{t, s} = \frac{\tilde{h}_t^T \cdot (\tilde{h}_s^S)^\top}{\tau_{\text{align}}}, \quad \tilde{h} = \frac{h}{\|h\|_2}, \quad \tau_{\text{align}} = 0.1.
\end{equation}
The target-to-source alignment posterior matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times S}$ and source token alignment confidence $c \in \mathbb{R}^S$ are defined as:
\begin{equation}
    A_{t, s} = \frac{\exp(M_{t, s})}{\sum_{s'=1}^S \exp(M_{t, s'})}, \quad c_s = \min\left(1.0, \sum_{t=1}^T A_{t, s}\right) \cdot \mathbb{I}(x_s \neq \texttt{<pad>}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{3. Residual Semantic Projector ($\Phi_\theta$)}
To prevent representation collapse, representations are projected through a non-linear residual network with LayerNorm:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_\theta(h) = \text{LayerNorm}(W_2 \cdot \text{GELU}(W_1 h + b_1) + b_2 + h),
\end{equation}
where $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{proj}} \times d_{\text{model}}}$, $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_{\text{proj}}}$, and $d_{\text{proj}} = 256$.

\section{Mathematical Formulation of Losses}
\label{sec:losses}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{1. Token-level Barycenter Anchoring Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}}$)}
For each source token $s$, we compute its corresponding target semantic barycenter $\bar{h}_s^T = \sum_{t=1}^T A_{t, s} h_t^T$. The structural loss regularizes source encoder states toward their target barycenters weighted by confidence $c_s$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}} = \frac{1}{\sum_s c_s + \epsilon} \sum_{s=1}^S c_s \cdot \text{Smooth}_{L1}\left(\Phi_\theta(h_s^S), \text{sg}(\Phi_\theta(\bar{h}_s^T))\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{2. Sentence-level Contrastive Priming Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}}$)}
Global sentence vectors are obtained by masked mean-pooling: $z^S = \text{MeanPool}(\Phi_\theta(h^S))$ and $z^T = \text{MeanPool}(\Phi_\theta(h^T))$. With in-batch negative sampling, the symmetric InfoNCE loss is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}} = -\frac{1}{2B} \sum_{i=1}^B \left[ \log \frac{\exp(z_i^S \cdot z_i^T / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(z_i^S \cdot z_j^T / \tau)} + \log \frac{\exp(z_i^T \cdot z_i^S / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(z_i^T \cdot z_j^S / \tau)} \right],
\end{equation}
where temperature $\tau = 0.07$ and teacher gradients are detached ($\text{sg}$).

\subsection{3. Dynamic Cross-Attention Routing Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{route}}$)}
At decoder layer $\ell$ and head $h$, the query state $q_{\ell h t}$ and pooled source summary $s_{\ell h} = W^s \text{MeanPool}(h^S)$ generate an adaptive scalar gate $g_{\ell h t} \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    g_{\ell h t} = \sigma\left(\text{MLP}\left([q_{\ell h t}; s_{\ell h}; q_{\ell h t} \odot s_{\ell h}]\right)\right).
\end{equation}
The gate is supervised by the cosine agreement target $r_{\ell h t}$ between the projected head representation $o_{\ell h t}$ and the teacher state $h_t^T$:
\begin{equation}
    r_{\ell h t} = \text{sg}\left(\frac{1 + \cos(R_h o_{\ell h t}, \Phi_\theta(h_t^T))}{2}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{route}} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}|} \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{\ell, h} \left[ r_{\ell h t} \log g_{\ell h t} + (1 - r_{\ell h t}) \log (1 - g_{\ell h t}) \right].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Total Multi-Task Objective \& Annealing Schedule}
The overall training objective combines translation cross-entropy with the three auxiliary terms:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MT}} + \lambda_1(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}} + \lambda_2(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}} + \lambda_3(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{route}}.
\end{equation}
The weights $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$ follow a calibrated three-phase schedule:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Phase 1 (Warmup, Epochs 1--2):} $\lambda_1(t): 0 \rightarrow 0.10, \lambda_2(t): 0 \rightarrow 0.05, \lambda_3 = 0.0$.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2 (Co-adaptation, Epochs 3--4):} $\lambda_1 = 0.10, \lambda_2 = 0.05, \lambda_3(t): 0 \rightarrow 0.10$.
    \item \textbf{Phase 3 (Fine-tuning, Epoch 5):} Fixed $(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3) = (0.10, 0.05, 0.10)$ with validation-based early stopping.
\end{itemize}

\section{Empirical Validation \& Experimental Results}
\label{sec:experimental_results}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Datasets \& Experimental Configuration}
Experiments are conducted on three distinct low-resource language pairs paired with Vietnamese:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Rhade (Ê Đê $\rightarrow$ Vi):} Austronesian family; 14,969 train pairs, 1,000 test pairs.
    \item \textbf{Tay (Tày $\rightarrow$ Vi):} Tai-Kadai family; 20,600 train pairs, 2,295 test pairs.
    \item \textbf{Bahnaric (Ba Na $\rightarrow$ Vi):} Mon-Khmer family; 51,900 train pairs, 2,001 test pairs.
\end{itemize}

All models are fine-tuned from \texttt{vinai/bartpho-syllable} for 5 epochs with batch size 16, learning rate $2\times 10^{-5}$ (AdamW, weight decay 0.01), sequence length 256, and mixed-precision FP16 on NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

\section{Summary of Technical Findings}
\label{sec:findings}
% ==============================================================================

The empirical results document the following technical conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Unanimous Superiority of TSSA 2.0:} Across all three distinct language families and across all four evaluation metrics (BLEU, chrF++, METEOR, COMET), TSSA 2.0 consistently outperforms the vanilla backbone and all four alignment paradigms.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Routing Preserves Fluency:} Gating cross-attention heads based on teacher cosine agreement prevents rigid alignment penalties from degrading target syntactic fluency.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Operation:} By deriving alignment posteriors on-the-fly from the Online Frozen Teacher in 0.001s, TSSA 2.0 operates without any external dependencies, providing a robust and reproducible foundation for low-resource translation.
\end{enumerate}
