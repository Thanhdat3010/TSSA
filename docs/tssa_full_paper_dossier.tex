\title{\textbf{Technical Documentation: Target-Side Semantic Anchoring (TSSA) for Low-Resource Neural Machine Translation}}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This technical report documents the theoretical formulation, architectural design, and empirical validation of \textbf{Target-Side Semantic Anchoring (TSSA)} for low-resource Neural Machine Translation (NMT). When fine-tuning massively pre-trained sequence-to-sequence backbones (BARTpho~\cite{tran2021bartpho}) on low-resource, typologically distant minority language pairs (15K--50K sentence pairs), standard cross-attention mechanisms suffer from severe attention sinking (up to 93\% of attention mass collapsing onto boundary tokens) and anisotropic representation distortion. Existing alignment techniques (e.g., token-level InfoNCE, autoregressive shift alignment, dependency graph supervision) degrade significantly in low-resource settings due to false-negative penalties, noisy pseudo-label confirmation bias, and lack of parser support.

TSSA provides an end-to-end, self-contained solution without requiring external parsers, statistical alignment tools, or offline disk caches. It unifies three core mechanisms: (1) an \textit{Online Frozen Target Teacher} extracting clean target-to-source alignment posteriors in real time (0.001s); (2) a \textit{Residual Semantic Projector} ($\Phi_\theta$) preserving latent geometry via non-linear residual mappings; and (3) a \textit{Dynamic Head Routing Gate} supervised by teacher agreement that decouples semantic translation heads from syntactic generation heads~\cite{voita2019analyzing}. 

Empirical evaluations across three distinct Vietnamese minority language families---Rhade (Austronesian, 15.1K pairs), Tay (Tai-Kadai, 20.6K pairs), and Bahnaric (Mon-Khmer, 51.9K pairs)---demonstrate that TSSA consistently outperforms the vanilla BARTpho baseline and four representative alignment paradigms (Align-to-Distill~\cite{jin2024align}, AWESOME-align~\cite{dou2021word}, Shift-AET~\cite{chen2020accurate}, and CL-LSA~\cite{chi2021infoxlm}) across all four standard metrics: SacreBLEU (+0.03 to +0.79)~\cite{post2018call}, chrF++ (+0.42 to +1.10)~\cite{popovic2015chrf}, METEOR (+0.31 to +0.90)~\cite{banerjee2005meteor}, and COMET (+0.0131 to +0.0414)~\cite{rei2020comet}. Intrinsic attention analysis confirms a 50.7\% reduction in cross-attention entropy and a $2.2\times$ increase in semantic keyword concentration.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem Formulation \& Core Challenges}
\label{sec:problem_formulation}
% ==============================================================================

In low-resource neural machine translation, we are given a parallel dataset $\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathcal{X}^{(k)}, \mathcal{Y}^{(k)})\}_{k=1}^{|\mathcal{D}|}$, where $\mathcal{X} = (x_1, \dots, x_S)$ is a source sentence in an under-resourced minority language and $\mathcal{Y} = (y_1, \dots, y_T)$ is the target sentence in Vietnamese. The parallel corpus size $|\mathcal{D}|$ is extremely constrained (ranging from 15,000 to 52,000 sentence pairs).

Under these data-scarce conditions and across non-isomorphic language families (Austronesian, Tai-Kadai, and Mon-Khmer paired with Vietnamese), standard sequence-to-sequence fine-tuning faces three fundamental challenges:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Attention Sinking \& Contextual Starvation:} Standard cross-attention $\alpha_{t, s} = \text{softmax}(q_t k_s^\top / \sqrt{d_k})$~\cite{vaswani2017attention,bahdanau2015neural} degenerates such that up to 90\%--93\% of attention mass is absorbed by special delimiter tokens (\texttt{<s>}, \texttt{<pad>}), leaving content words ungrounded.
    \item \textbf{Representation Collapse in High-Dimensional Spaces:} High-dimensional embeddings ($d_{\text{model}} = 1024$) fine-tuned on limited samples undergo anisotropic collapse, where sentence representations occupy a narrow cone in representation space.
    \item \textbf{Failure of Existing Alignment Paradigms on Low-Resource Data:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textit{Token-level InfoNCE (CL-LSA)~\cite{chi2021infoxlm}:} Treating all other in-batch tokens as negative instances severely penalizes morphological variants and synonyms (false negative penalty), degrading performance ($17.85$ BLEU on Rhade, $4.49$ on Bahnaric).
        \item \textit{Shifted Autoregressive States (Shift-AET)~\cite{chen2020accurate}:} Symmetrized pseudo-alignment labels extracted from unregularized models contain substantial noise, causing error propagation during autoregressive decoding ($19.44$ BLEU on Tay vs. $24.67$ baseline).
        \item \textit{Syntactic Supervision~\cite{li2022structural}:} Low-resource minority languages lack robust dependency parsers, introducing corrupted graph structures.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Architecture: TSSA}
\label{sec:system_architecture}
% ==============================================================================

TSSA addresses these challenges through a fully integrated, multi-task sequence-to-sequence framework built on top of the pre-trained BARTpho-syllable backbone~\cite{tran2021bartpho} ($d_{\text{model}} = 1024, H = 16, L_{\text{enc}} = L_{\text{dec}} = 6$).

\subsection{Online Frozen Target Teacher ($\mathcal{E}_T$)}
Instead of relying on external aligners (LaBSE, GIZA++, FastAlign) or static disk caches, TSSA embeds a parameter-frozen copy of the target language encoder:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{E}_T = \text{stop\_gradient}(f_{\text{enc}}), \quad h^T = \mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{Y}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times d_{\text{model}}}.
\end{equation}
Because the student decoder and teacher encoder share the identical Vietnamese tokenizer and vocabulary, target position $t$ in the teacher maps directly to position $t$ in the decoder without tokenization mismatches.

\subsection{Dynamic Posterior Alignment Matrix ($A$)}
During training, the student encoder outputs source states $h^S = f_{\text{enc}}(\mathcal{X}) \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times d_{\text{model}}}$. The cross-lingual token similarity matrix $M \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times S}$ is computed on-the-fly:
\begin{equation}
    M_{t, s} = \frac{\tilde{h}_t^T \cdot (\tilde{h}_s^S)^\top}{\tau_{\text{align}}}, \quad \tilde{h} = \frac{h}{\|h\|_2}, \quad \tau_{\text{align}} = 0.1.
\end{equation}
The target-to-source alignment posterior matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times S}$ and source token alignment confidence $c \in \mathbb{R}^S$ are defined as:
\begin{equation}
    A_{t, s} = \frac{\exp(M_{t, s})}{\sum_{s'=1}^S \exp(M_{t, s'})}, \quad c_s = \min\left(1.0, \sum_{t=1}^T A_{t, s}\right) \cdot \mathbb{I}(x_s \neq \texttt{<pad>}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Residual Semantic Projector ($\Phi_\theta$)}
To prevent representation collapse, representations are projected through a non-linear residual network with LayerNorm:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_\theta(h) = \text{LayerNorm}(W_2 \cdot \text{GELU}(W_1 h + b_1) + b_2 + h),
\end{equation}
where $W_1 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{proj}} \times d_{\text{model}}}$, $W_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{model}} \times d_{\text{proj}}}$, $d_{\text{model}} = 1024$, and $d_{\text{proj}} = 2048$.

\section{Mathematical Formulation of Losses}
\label{sec:losses}
% ==============================================================================

\subsection{Token-level Barycenter Anchoring Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}}$)}
For each source token $s$, we compute its corresponding target semantic barycenter $\bar{h}_s^T = \sum_{t=1}^T A_{t, s} h_t^T$. The structural loss regularizes source encoder states toward their target barycenters weighted by confidence $c_s$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}} = \frac{1}{\sum_s c_s + \epsilon} \sum_{s=1}^S c_s \cdot \text{Smooth}_{L1}\left(\Phi_\theta(h_s^S), \text{sg}(\Phi_\theta(\bar{h}_s^T))\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sentence-level Contrastive Priming Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}}$)}
Global sentence vectors are obtained by masked mean-pooling: $z^S = \text{MeanPool}(\Phi_\theta(h^S))$ and $z^T = \text{MeanPool}(\Phi_\theta(h^T))$. With in-batch negative sampling, the symmetric InfoNCE loss is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}} = -\frac{1}{2B} \sum_{i=1}^B \left[ \log \frac{\exp(z_i^S \cdot z_i^T / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(z_i^S \cdot z_j^T / \tau)} + \log \frac{\exp(z_i^T \cdot z_i^S / \tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^B \exp(z_i^T \cdot z_j^S / \tau)} \right],
\end{equation}
where temperature $\tau = 0.07$ and teacher gradients are detached ($\text{sg}$).

\subsection{Dynamic Cross-Attention Routing Loss ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{route}}$)}
At decoder layer $\ell$ and head $h$, the query state $q_{\ell h t}$ and pooled source summary $s_{\ell h} = W^s \text{MeanPool}(h^S)$ generate an adaptive scalar gate $g_{\ell h t} \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
    g_{\ell h t} = \sigma\left(\text{MLP}\left([q_{\ell h t}; s_{\ell h}; q_{\ell h t} \odot s_{\ell h}]\right)\right).
\end{equation}
The gate is supervised by the cosine agreement target $r_{\ell h t}$ between the projected head representation $o_{\ell h t}$ and the teacher state $h_t^T$:
\begin{equation}
    r_{\ell h t} = \text{sg}\left(\frac{1 + \cos(R_h o_{\ell h t}, \Phi_\theta(h_t^T))}{2}\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{route}} = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Y}|} \sum_{t=1}^T \sum_{\ell, h} \left[ r_{\ell h t} \log g_{\ell h t} + (1 - r_{\ell h t}) \log (1 - g_{\ell h t}) \right].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Total Multi-Task Objective \& Annealing Schedule}
The overall training objective combines translation cross-entropy with the three auxiliary terms:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{MT}} + \lambda_1(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}} + \lambda_2(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}} + \lambda_3(t) \mathcal{L}_{\text{route}}.
\end{equation}
The weights $\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$ follow a calibrated three-phase schedule:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Phase 1 (Warmup, Epochs 1--2):} $\lambda_1(t): 0 \rightarrow 0.10, \lambda_2(t): 0 \rightarrow 0.05, \lambda_3 = 0.0$.
    \item \textbf{Phase 2 (Co-adaptation, Epochs 3--4):} $\lambda_1 = 0.10, \lambda_2 = 0.05, \lambda_3(t): 0 \rightarrow 0.10$.
    \item \textbf{Phase 3 (Fine-tuning, Epoch 5):} Fixed $(\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3) = (0.10, 0.05, 0.10)$ with validation-based early stopping.
\end{itemize}

Experiments are conducted on three distinct low-resource language pairs paired with Vietnamese:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]

\paragraph{Statistical Significance Analysis.}
To rigorously assess whether the performance improvements of TSSA are statistically distinguishable from random variation, we conducted Paired Bootstrap Resampling~\cite{koehn2004statistical} with $B=1,000$ iterations (seed 42) across both SacreBLEU and chrF++ against both the vanilla backbone and the strongest baseline per language.
As annotated in Table~\ref{tab:main_benchmark_3langs}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Rhade:} TSSA achieves statistically significant superiority over Vanilla BARTpho in both SacreBLEU ($p = 0.0130 < 0.05$, 95\% CI $[+0.08, +1.32]$) and chrF++ ($p < 0.001$, 95\% CI $[+0.57, +1.68]$), while decisively outperforming the strongest baseline (AWESOME-align) at $p < 0.001$ across both metrics (denoted by $^{\dagger\ddagger}$).
    \item \textbf{Tay:} Gains over Vanilla BARTpho are statistically verified at $p < 0.05$ for both SacreBLEU ($p = 0.0390$) and chrF++ ($p = 0.0410$), denoted by $^\dagger$.
    \item \textbf{Bahnaric:} On this challenging Mon-Khmer language, character n-gram coverage (chrF++) significantly outperforms Vanilla BARTpho ($p = 0.0140 < 0.05$, denoted by $^\dagger$, 95\% CI $[+0.05, +0.83]$), while simultaneously demonstrating strict statistical dominance over the strongest baseline Align-to-Distill ($p = 0.0020 < 0.01$ on SacreBLEU and $p < 0.001$ on chrF++, denoted by $^\ddagger$).
\end{itemize}

The ablation results provide several crucial insights into the interaction between alignment objectives and language typologies:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Resolution of N-Gram Bias via Neural Metrics (Rhade):} In Rhade, surface SacreBLEU marginally increases (+0.13) when ablating $\mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}}$. However, neural COMET drops substantially from $-0.3264$ to $-0.3346$ ($-0.0082$), and chrF++ decreases from $40.43$ to $40.36$. This illustrates a classic phenomenon in low-resource generation where unregularized decoders generate repetitive high-frequency $n$-grams that inflate surface BLEU while degrading deep semantic faithfulness. Full TSSA achieves the highest neural semantic fidelity and character coverage.
    \item \textbf{Structural Anchoring as Anchor for Isomorphic Syntax (Tay):} In Tay, which shares strict SVO word order with Vietnamese, removing token barycenter anchoring ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{struct}}$) induces the sharpest drop across all variants ($-0.19$ BLEU, COMET dropping to $-0.5169$). Token-to-token semantic alignment is paramount for syntactically isomorphic low-resource pairs.
    \item \textbf{Contrastive Priming as Lifeline for Distant Typology (Bahnaric):} In Bahnaric (Mon-Khmer), ablating sentence contrastive priming ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{prime}}$) triggers a catastrophic performance drop of \textbf{-0.47 BLEU} ($9.66 \rightarrow 9.19$), $-0.46$ chrF++, and $-0.0230$ COMET (falling below vanilla BARTpho). In highly disparate language pairs with noisy subword representations, global sentence priming is essential to prevent anisotropic representation collapse.
\end{enumerate}

TSSA precisely captures the intended community governance semantics as \textit{``sự đoàn kết''} (solidarity, chrF++: $\mathbf{100.0}$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Summary of Technical Findings}
\label{sec:findings}
% ==============================================================================

The empirical results document the following technical conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Consistent Superiority of TSSA:} Across all three distinct language families and across all four evaluation metrics (BLEU, chrF++, METEOR, COMET), TSSA consistently outperforms the vanilla backbone and all four alignment paradigms.
    \item \textbf{Decoupled Routing Preserves Fluency:} Gating cross-attention heads based on teacher cosine agreement prevents rigid alignment penalties from degrading target syntactic fluency.
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Operation:} By deriving alignment posteriors on-the-fly from the Online Frozen Teacher in 0.001s, TSSA operates without any external dependencies, providing a robust foundation for low-resource translation.
\end{enumerate}
